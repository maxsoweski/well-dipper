# B — Hierarchical / Multi-Resolution Procedural Drainage (prior art)

Scope: how to keep river networks hydrologically consistent across scales, and how to refine a coarse trunk network into finer tributaries. Sources verified from primary PDFs (equations quoted from the papers themselves).

## The two quantitative laws that set tributary counts & channel widths at a scale
- **Hack's law** — main-stream length vs basin area: **L = C·A^h**, with **h ≈ 0.57** (empirically "slightly less than 0.6"; range ~0.5–0.7). Implies river networks are statistically **self-similar / fractal** → the *same* branching statistics recur at every zoom level. This is *why* view-dependent refinement is legitimate: a sub-basin looks like a scaled copy of its parent.
- **Discharge vs drainage area (Dunne–Leopold power law)** — Génevaux uses **φ = 0.42·A^0.69** (mean flow [m³/s], A = watershed area [m²]). A is just the summed area of upstream cells (you already compute this via flow accumulation).
- **Hydraulic geometry (Leopold–Maddock 1953)** — channel **width w ∝ Q^b** with **b ≈ 0.5** (depth f≈0.4, velocity m≈0.1; b+f+m=1). Chaining: **w ∝ A^(0.69·0.5) ≈ A^0.35**. So width grows roughly as the *third-root-ish* of drainage area — trunks are wide, headwaters thin, and a channel of Strahler order s is ~3× narrower than order s+1.
- **Drainage density** sets the *finest* channel spacing: real terrains have a characteristic hillslope-to-channel length. Below that scale you stop adding channels and switch to unchannelized hillslope. This is the physical cap on "how thin / how close."

**Net answer to "how many tributaries / how thin at a given viewing scale":** Strahler self-similarity + Hack's law fix the *count and length* of sub-tributaries you must add to fill a basin to a target resolution; the width law (w∝A^~0.35) fixes ribbon thickness from accumulated area; drainage density fixes the stopping resolution. All three are scale-relative → drive them off pixels-per-km at the current camera distance.

## Is there an established technique to refine a coarse network into a finer one consistently? Yes — three lineages.
1. **Génevaux et al. 2013 (SIGGRAPH/ToG)** — grows the network with an **L-system / grammar** over a geometric graph. Nonterminals carry the **Horton–Strahler number s**; rules are: continuation (Pc), **symmetric junction → two order-(s−1) branches** (Ps), **asymmetric junction → order-s + order-m, m<s** (Pa). New nodes must be **higher than their parent** (guarantees downhill flow), inside the domain, and kept apart by **σ·f(s)** — spacing *scales with Strahler order* so big rivers stay far apart and small ones pack denser. ζ controls ramification density. **This is exactly a recursive refinement recipe** and maps onto an existing coarse graph.
2. **Cordonnier et al. 2016 (CGF) — uplift + fluvial erosion.** Solves the **stream power equation: dh/dt = u(p) − k·A(p)^m·s(p)^n**, with **m=0.5, n=1, m/n≈0.5**. Builds a **stream tree** (each node flows to lowest neighbor = receiver) over a Poisson-sampled Delaunay graph; A = drainage area. **Your existing pipeline (priority-flood + D-inf flow accumulation + Strahler) IS this stream graph** — you already have A and the trunk topology. Cordonnier shows hydrologically-consistent **blending between patches** and inverse recovery of uplift from a DEM. Refinement = re-run on a denser graph seeded by the coarse elevations.
3. **Dendry (Gaillard et al. 2019, I3D)** — the most directly relevant to *view-dependent* refinement. A **locally computable** procedural function **f(p) = distance to an implicitly-constructed branching tree T**; evaluable at any point **and any scale**, parallel, tiny memory, no need to store the whole tree. A **global control function c:ℝ²→[0,1]** defines the coarse shape; **each subdivision level adds finer branches that inherit the coarse structure** (their Fig. 1 = exactly coarse-trunk → level-2 → level-3 tributaries). Designed to produce **hydrologically consistent terrain heightfields with river networks**. This is the textbook "synthesize fine tributaries that agree with a coarse trunk" primitive.

## Planetary / sphere-specific
- **Derzapf et al. 2011, "River Networks for Instant Procedural Planets" (CGF)** — defines **coarse altitudes + a base river network on a planet, then refines it in REAL TIME by a subdivision process during fly-through** (adaptive, view-dependent, no preprocessing). This is the closest published analog to the spike's goal (spacecraft → close approach). (Derzapf full-text was access-blocked; method summary corroborated via Gaillard 2019's related-work and multiple abstracts.)

## Decision-relevant takeaways for the spike
- Don't rebuild the global solver. Treat the existing 40k-vertex priority-flood result as the **coarse trunk graph**; the literature's job is *adding sub-tributaries below it*.
- Two viable refinement engines: **(a) Dendry-style local procedural function** (best for true view-dependent, per-pixel/streaming, no global state) or **(b) Génevaux grammar / Cordonnier re-sim on a locally densified patch** (best fidelity, but needs a local mesh). Dendry is the lower-risk spike target because it's *locally computable at arbitrary scale by construction*.
- Consistency guarantees come for free if refinement obeys: child elevation > parent (downhill), Strahler-scaled spacing (σ·f(s)), and width = A^~0.35. Self-similarity (Hack) means the coarse-scale statistics are the *correct* statistics to replicate at fine scale.
