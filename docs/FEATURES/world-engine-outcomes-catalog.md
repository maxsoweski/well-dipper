## Additions by domain

### Volcanic / thermal / magmatic (14)

- **Active eruption plumes (umbrella/curtain plumes)** · `OBS` — Visible ballistic eruption columns rising off the surface during an eruption, NOT a cloud-physics feature. Io's Pele- and Prometheus-class plumes loft sulfur/SO2 + pyroclastics 100-500 km up in umbrella shapes; Triton's nitrogen geysers loft dark plumes ~8 km; Enceladus's south-polar jets. The baseline has clouds (F31), dust storms (F40), and lightning (F30) as atmospheric phenomena, but NO surface-sourced eruption column. This is the single most-recognizable 'this planet is volcanically alive' visual and it is absent. Observed on Io (Voyager/Galileo/Juno), Triton (Voyager 2), Enceladus (Cassini).
  - bodies: Io (Pele umbrella plume, Tvashtar curtain, Prometheus), Triton (dark nitrogen geyser plumes), Enceladus (south-polar tiger-stripe jets) · engine→ _Active-volcanism / endogenic-heat engine (mantle/tidal heat -> melt -> eruptive venting); the same engine drives F7/F8 but in its ACTIVE-eruption mode rather than its cooled-landform mode_ · src: Wikipedia 'Volcanism on Io' (Pele/Prometheus/Tvashtar plume types, sulfur/SO2 frost rings); Geophysical Research Letters, Rathbun et al. 2002 'Loki, Io: A periodic volcano'; NASA/JPL PIA14449 'Triton's Volcanic Plumes' (Voyager 2); Cassini Enceladus plume imaging (Porco et al. 2006, Science)
- **Lava fountains & curtains of fire (fissure fountaining)** · `OBS` — Incandescent lava jetted non-explosively from a vent or along a fissure: a point fountain (Hawaiian) or a linear 'curtain of fire' along a graben/fissure (Laki, Holuhraun on Earth; Tvashtar's 25-km-long curtain on Io; inferred at Cerberus Fossae on Mars). Distinct from a plume (ballistic ash/gas column) and from a cooled lava channel (F8): this is the bright, line-or-point emission of fresh molten rock at the active vent. Absent from baseline.
  - bodies: Earth (Laki 1783 25-km fissure, Holuhraun 2014, Kilauea Hawaiian fountains), Io (Tvashtar Catena 25-km curtain of fire, Galileo 1999), Mars (Cerberus Fossae, inferred fissure-fed flows) · engine→ _Active-volcanism / endogenic-heat engine in fissure-effusive mode (dike reaches surface along a fault -> linear vent)_ · src: USGS 'Fall into a burning curtain of fire with fissure eruptions'; Wikipedia 'Laki' and 'Holuhraun'; ScienceDirect, Keszthelyi et al. 2001/2003 'The final Galileo SSI observations of Io' (Tvashtar curtain); Wikipedia 'Cerberus Fossae' (fissure vents surrounded by flow features)
- **Active lava lake (overturning crust, glowing margin)** · `OBS` — A persistent pool of molten rock in a vent/patera with a chilled dark crust broken by glowing incandescent cracks and a hot margin where crust founders. Visually: dark plates separated by bright orange lines, hottest at the rim. Io's Loki Patera (202 km, periodic ~540-day overturn) is the giant; Earth has Erta Ale, Nyiragongo, Kilauea's Halemaumau, Mt Erebus. The baseline's F41 hemispheric magma ocean is a whole-dayside extreme; a discrete lava lake inside a caldera is a different, far-more-common scale and is missing.
  - bodies: Io (Loki Patera, Pele lava lake with fountaining margin), Earth (Erta Ale, Nyiragongo, Halemaumau, Mt Erebus) · engine→ _Active-volcanism / endogenic-heat engine in standing-melt (lava-lake) mode; thermal-emission rendering shares the F32/F33 self-emission shader_ · src: Wikipedia 'Loki Patera' (202 km lava lake, episodic crustal overturn); Geophysical Research Letters, Rathbun et al. 2002 'Loki, Io: A periodic volcano' (540-day period); ScienceDirect, Davies et al. 2012 'Temperature and variability of Pillan, Wayland Patera, and Loki Patera on Io from Cassini ISS'
- **Fresh-vs-aged flow thermal/albedo mosaic (active flow fields)** · `OBS` — On an active body the surface is a patchwork of flows at different ages: glowing/dark fresh silicate flows next to cooling flows being progressively buried under bright sulfur snow (Io), or dark fresh basalt over older weathered flows (Earth/Mars/Venus). The visual is a high-contrast color/temperature mottling of the volcanic plain. The baseline F8 treats lava plains as one static texture; the time-progressive thermal+albedo mosaic that signals ongoing activity is absent.
  - bodies: Io (dark fresh silicate flows vs yellow-red sulfur-frosted older flows), Earth (dark recent basalt over vegetated/weathered older flows), Venus (radar-dark/bright flow units of different ages over coronae) · engine→ _Active-volcanism engine + surface-aging/weathering engine acting jointly (emplacement age field -> albedo + residual-heat field)_ · src: Wikipedia 'Volcanism on Io' (fresh silicate flows in stages of sulfur-snow burial); Universe Today 'First Ever Geologic Map of Io: 425 Volcanoes' (Williams et al. USGS map); Solarviews 'Venusian Volcanic Features'
- **Volcanic plume/pyroclastic deposit rings (surface coloration halos)** · `OBS` — The painted-on consequence of plumes: concentric albedo rings of deposited material around a vent. Io's Pele leaves a ~1300-km red sulfur ring with a dark silicate center; Prometheus-type plumes leave bright white SO2-frost rings; dark pyroclastic blankets ('dark mantle deposits') ring lunar/Mercurian explosive vents. Distinct from impact ejecta/rays (F3) because the source is a volcanic vent and the chemistry (sulfur reds, SO2 whites, dark glass) is diagnostic. Absent from baseline.
  - bodies: Io (Pele red ring + black center, Prometheus white SO2 rings), Moon (regional dark mantle deposits, e.g. Aristarchus/Sulpicius Gallus), Mercury (pyroclastic dark/red 'faculae' deposits) · engine→ _Active-volcanism engine (explosive/plume mode) -> ballistic deposition field painting a radial albedo/color overlay_ · src: Wikipedia 'Volcanism on Io' (Pele red ring, plume frost rings, red/black/white painting); Volcano World (Oregon State) 'Dark Mantling Deposits' (lunar dark-halo + regional deposits); VolcanoCafe 'A quick tour of volcanism on Io'
- **Lunar/Mercurian volcanic resurfaced plains (mare basalt fill, smooth plains)** · `OBS` — Vast smooth low-albedo basalt plains that flooded and buried older cratered terrain (lunar maria; Mercury's northern smooth plains; Caloris interior plains). The diagnostic visual is a sharp embayment contact where dark smooth lava laps against and partly drowns bright older highlands and craters, plus 'ghost/flooded craters' showing through. Baseline F8 (lava plains & flows) reads as channels/flows on a body; the planet-darkening flood-resurfacing-as-a-tonal-province (mare vs highland dichotomy) and the embayment/ghost-crater signature are not represented.
  - bodies: Moon (nearside maria vs highlands dichotomy, ghost craters in Mare Imbrium), Mercury (northern smooth plains, Caloris floor), Mars (Hesperian ridged plains) · engine→ _Flood-volcanism / large-igneous-province engine (high-effusion basalt floods topographic lows -> resurfacing province); same engine as F-relief F8 but at province/global tonal scale_ · src: Wikipedia 'Volcanism on the Moon' (maria, embayment, ghost craters); EBSCO Research Starters 'Lunar maria'; Lancaster eprints, 'Lunar Mare Basaltic Volcanism' (New Views of the Moon-2, Ch.11)
- **Floor-fractured craters & irregular mare patches (volcanically-modified craters)** · `OBS` — Impact craters later modified by magmatic intrusion: their floors uplifted and broken into a polygonal fracture pattern (floor-fractured craters, e.g. lunar Posidonius, Gassendi), sometimes with dark pyroclastic ponds. Irregular mare patches (IMPs) are small, young, blistery/rough-and-smooth volcanic spots (e.g. lunar Ina). The visual is a crater whose interior reads as cracked/intruded rather than a clean impact bowl. This crosses F2 (craters) x volcanism and is in neither cleanly.
  - bodies: Moon (floor-fractured craters Posidonius, Gassendi, Humboldt; irregular mare patch Ina; Marius Hills domes), Mars (some intra-crater volcanic fills) · engine→ _Magmatic-intrusion engine (sill/laccolith emplaced under a pre-existing crater floor -> uplift + fracture); a crater-modification overlay, not a fresh-crater engine_ · src: Wikipedia 'Volcanism on the Moon' (floor-fractured craters, irregular mare patches, Marius Hills); LRO/NASA 2014 announcement of 70 irregular mare patches (Braden et al. 2014, Nature Geoscience); ScienceDirect, Wohler et al. 2007 'Formation of lunar mare domes along crustal fractures'
- **Cryovolcanic flow plains & cryolava domes (viscous icy effusion)** · `OBS` — The cryo-analogue of effusive volcanism: smooth resurfacing plains of frozen cryolava (Triton's Cipango Planum), thick steep-sided cryolava domes (Ceres' Ahuna Mons, Pluto's Wright/Piccard Mons, Titan's Doom Mons flank flows), and 'guttae'/walled-plain cryoflows. Toothpaste-consistency ammonia-water or N2-CH4 melt. Baseline F7 lists cryovolcanism only inside the corona/edifice bullet; the distinctively low-albedo-OR-bright, lobate, viscous CRYOLAVA SHEET and CRYODOME morphologies (and their resurfacing of older terrain) are not broken out.
  - bodies: Ceres (Ahuna Mons cryodome), Pluto (Wright Mons, Piccard Mons, large-scale cryovolcanic resurfacing), Titan (Doom Mons flank flows), Triton (Cipango Planum smooth cryoplain) · engine→ _Cryovolcanism engine (subsurface-ocean overpressure / diapiric melt -> low-temperature volatile effusion); a parallel low-temperature copy of the active-volcanism engine_ · src: Nature Communications, Singer et al. 2022 'Large-scale cryovolcanic resurfacing on Pluto'; ScienceDirect, Cruikshank et al. 2020 'Ammonia-water freezing as a mechanism for recent cryovolcanism on Pluto'; NASA Dawn / Ruesch et al. 2016 'Cryovolcanism on Ceres' (Ahuna Mons, Science); Wikipedia 'Geology of Triton' (Cipango Planum); arXiv:2310.10904 'Kiladze Caldera: A Possible Cryovolcano on Pluto'
- **Cantaloupe / diapiric terrain (solid-state convective resurfacing)** · `OBS` — Triton's signature 'cantaloupe terrain': a field of closely-packed dimpled depressions (cavi) 30-50 km wide separated by ridges, attributed to rising solid-state diapirs of buoyant ice — an endogenic, magmatic-adjacent resurfacing. Visually a knobbly cantaloupe-rind texture unlike chaos (F9) or grooved ice (F10). The baseline lenticulae/diapir mention sits inside F10 (Europan ridged ice); Triton's distinct cantaloupe pattern is not separately captured.
  - bodies: Triton (cantaloupe terrain / cavi), (loosely) icy-shell lenticulae fields elsewhere · engine→ _Solid-state diapirism / icy-mantle-overturn engine (buoyant ice diapirs deform the lithosphere from below — thermal/compositional convection, a cryo-magmatic cousin)_ · src: Wikipedia 'Geology of Triton' (cantaloupe terrain, diapir interpretation); Labroots 'Triton: The Cantaloupe Moon'; Schenk & Jackson 1993 'Diapirism on Triton' (Geology)
- **Hotspot edifice chains / shield-fields (mantle-plume tracks)** · `OBS` — Linear or clustered chains of volcanic edifices recording a fixed mantle plume under a moving (or static) lithosphere: Hawaii-Emperor seamount chain on Earth; Tharsis Montes alignment and Elysium on Mars; Marius Hills shield clusters on the Moon; possible Venus hotspot fields. Visual: a row/cluster of progressively-aged shields, freshest at the active end. The SCOPE explicitly names 'hotspot tracks' but the baseline F7 has no chain/lineation construct — edifices are individual, not plume-track-organized.
  - bodies: Earth (Hawaii-Emperor chain), Mars (Tharsis Montes trio: Arsia/Pavonis/Ascraeus alignment), Moon (Marius Hills shield field) · engine→ _Mantle-plume / hotspot engine (deep stationary thermal upwelling + lithospheric motion -> age-progressive edifice lineament)_ · src: AGU JGR Planets, Mouginis-Mark 2005 'New observations of volcanic features on Mars from THEMIS'; Grokipedia/standard refs 'Volcanism on Mars' (Tharsis Montes alignment); Wikipedia 'Volcanism on the Moon' (Marius Hills shield field)
- **Lava-tube skylight chains & collapsed-tube pit crater rows** · `OBS` — Chains of roughly circular pits marking a partially-collapsed lava tube: open skylights (a deep shadowed hole with a sunlit boulder-strewn floor) progressing to fully-collapsed segmented troughs. Common on Mars (Tharsis, Pavonis), the Moon (Marius Hills rilles), and Earth. Baseline F8 mentions 'collapsed tube-pit chain' inside the lava-plains bullet, but the SKYLIGHT (intact tube with a single roof hole — the lava-cave/habitat target) and the visual distinction between open-skylight vs collapsed-trough stages are not separately represented.
  - bodies: Mars (Pavonis Mons skylights, Tharsis pit chains, Hephaestus Fossae), Moon (Marius Hills rille skylight, Mare Tranquillitatis pit), Earth (Hawaiian/Icelandic lava-tube skylights) · engine→ _Effusive-volcanism engine, tube-roofing sub-process (insulated tube flow -> roof + later collapse); same engine as F8 lava channels, different surface expression_ · src: AGU JGR Planets, Mouginis-Mark 2005 'New observations of volcanic features on Mars from THEMIS' (skylights, pit chains); arXiv:2004.13156 'Prospective Lava Tubes at Hellas Planitia'; imars.uwo.ca 'Interactive Mapping of Mars: volcanic'
- **Hydrovolcanic / phreatomagmatic cones (maars, tuff rings, rootless cones)** · `OBS` — Landforms from magma-water interaction: maars (broad low crater BELOW ground level, often water-filled, ringed by a low tephra rim), tuff rings/cones (crater ABOVE ground level), and rootless cones / 'pseudocraters' (cones with no magma source, formed where a lava sheet flowed over wet ground and steam-exploded — abundant on Mars in Athabasca/Cerberus and in Iceland). Visual: clusters of small low rimmed pits, often in groups on a lava plain. Baseline F7 lists shield/strato/caldera/pancake/corona but no small monogenetic / hydrovolcanic cone class.
  - bodies: Earth (Hverfjall tuff ring Iceland, Eifel maars Germany, Ukinrek maars), Mars (Athabasca Valles rootless cone fields / pseudocraters, Cerberus), Iceland (Myvatn pseudocraters) · engine→ _Phreatomagmatic / hydrovolcanic engine (magma + groundwater/ice -> steam explosion -> tephra ring); a water-gated branch of the volcanism engine_ · src: NPS 'Maars and Tuff Rings'; Volcano World (Oregon State) 'Hydrovolcanic Landforms'; Nature npj Space Exploration 2026 'Recent explosive lava-water interaction in Tharsis, Mars'; AGU JGR Planets, Mouginis-Mark 2005 (rootless cones from lava over wet surface)
- **Cinder/scoria cone & spatter cone fields (small monogenetic cones)** · `OBS` — Fields of small steep conical hills of loose tephra built around a single vent in one eruptive episode, frequently with a summit crater and a breached-flank lava apron; spatter cones are smaller and welded. They cluster in volcanic fields (e.g. San Francisco volcanic field, Tenerife, Tharsis low shields/cones). The smallest and most numerous volcano type, and a strong 'scatter many small cones across a plain' visual primitive — entirely missing from baseline F7 (which jumps from shields to coronae).
  - bodies: Earth (Paricutin, San Francisco volcanic field, Tenerife cinder cones), Mars (Tharsis cinder cones and low shields), Moon (mare cones, e.g. in Marius Hills) · engine→ _Monogenetic-volcanism engine (single short-lived basaltic eruption -> tephra cone); the low-volume end-member of the same effusive/explosive engine as F7 shields_ · src: NPS 'Types of Volcanoes' and 'Anatomy of a Volcano'; Wikipedia 'Volcanic cone'; ScienceDirect, Dohrenwend-type 'Volcanic geomorphological classification of the cinder cones of Tenerife'; Grokipedia 'Volcanism on Mars' (Tharsis low shields and cones)
- **Columnar-jointed basalt & lava-flow surface textures (pahoehoe/aa/blocky)** · `OBS` — Close-range surface textures of cooled lava: hexagonal columnar jointing (Giant's Causeway, Devils Tower, Deccan rosettes), ropy/smooth pahoehoe, rubbly clinkery aa, and blocky flow tops. These are LOD2 close-up textures distinguishing a lava surface from sediment or regolith. The baseline lists lava plains (F8) and crystalline/hex EXOTIC fields (F43/F44) but conflates natural columnar basalt jointing (a real, common cooling texture) with the speculative crystalline/hex-crust features; the ordinary basalt surface texture set is missing.
  - bodies: Earth (Giant's Causeway columnar basalt, Deccan Traps rosettes, Hawaiian pahoehoe/aa), Mars (inferred columnar jointing in crater walls / Marte Vallis), Moon (mare flow surfaces) · engine→ _Effusive-volcanism engine, cooling/solidification sub-process (thermal-contraction jointing + flow-surface rheology -> texture); a close-LOD texture channel on F8_ · src: Geology Base 'Get the Six Lava Flow Types' and 'Aa Lava Flow' / 'Pahoehoe'; Sandatlas 'Types of Lava Flows'; ScienceDirect, Sheth et al. 2017 'Breccia-cored columnar rosettes...Deccan Traps'

### Fluvial / lacustrine / coastal (13)

- **Inverted / exhumed channels (sinuous fluvial ridges)** · `OBS` — Former river channels that now stand as POSITIVE relief ridges, not valleys. The channel-floor sediment was indurated (cemented, clast-armored, or lava-filled) so it resisted erosion while the softer surroundings were stripped, producing relief inversion. This is the single most under-represented fluvial outcome in the baseline: F11/F13 model rivers as incised lows, but a huge fraction of preserved Mars fluvial record (Jezero/Eberswalde fan channels, Aeolis Dorsa 'great river deposits') is sinuous RIDGES. Same drainage planform (dendritic, meandering, distributary) but inverted sign of relief. Should be a rendering MODE on the drainage engine, gated by a surface-age / exhumation driver, not a separate landform.
  - bodies: Mars (Aeolis Dorsa sinuous ridges; Jezero western fan inverted channels; Eberswalde distributaries), Earth (inverted paleochannels, Western Desert of Egypt; Green River / Utah channel belts) · engine→ _Drainage / stream-power network engine (same as F11) with an added 'relief-inversion sign-flip' stage driven by induration + differential-erosion / exhumation state (surface-history D11/D16)._ · src: Williams et al. 2018, 'Thermophysical Properties of Martian Fluvial Sinuous Ridges,' Earth and Space Science (Wiley); Hayden & Lamb 2018, 'Formation of sinuous ridges by inversion of river-channel belts in Utah, USA, with implications for Mars,' Icarus; Planetary Geomorphology Image of the Month, 'Inverted Paleochannels on Earth and Mars' (planetarygeomorphology.wordpress.com, 2013)
- **Fluvial terraces (paired / unpaired / strath / fill)** · `OBS` — Bench-like steps flanking a valley, recording successive abandoned floodplain levels left as a river incises after a base-level fall, uplift, or climate shift. Strath terraces = bedrock surfaces laterally planed then veneered with thin alluvium; fill terraces = cut into earlier valley-fill. Paired terraces sit at equal height on both banks (synchronous incision); unpaired terraces alternate (lateral migration during incision). They are the visible RECORD of the incision history / base-level staircase. Baseline F11 has only 'meanders / single trunk / relict' — it has no terrace/incision-stage axis at all.
  - bodies: Mars (paired and unpaired river terraces; inner-gorge terraces in valley networks), Earth (river terrace staircases worldwide) · engine→ _Drainage / valley-incision engine coupled to a base-level + uplift driver — the same stream-power incision (dh/dt = u - k*A^m*S^n) that cuts the valley, but with episodic base-level steps producing terrace flights._ · src: Limaye & Lamb 2016, 'Numerical model predictions of autogenic fluvial terraces,' JGR Earth Surface (Wiley); Mars fluvial-landform inventories noting 'paired and unpaired river terraces, incised inner gorges and/or fluvial knickpoints' (multiple Mars geomorphology reviews)
- **Knickpoints, inner gorges & waterfall plunge-pool heads** · `OBS` — Abrupt steepenings (knickpoints) in a river's long profile — convex breaks marking a wave of incision propagating upstream after a base-level drop. Where the knickpoint stalls or cuts hard rock it forms a waterfall + plunge pool; sustained retreat carves an amphitheater-headed (theater-headed) valley head and a steep INNER GORGE nested within a wider older valley. These are the discrete markers of WHERE a river is actively eating headward, i.e. the system's response to base-level change made visible as a single feature. Absent from baseline entirely.
  - bodies: Mars (fluvial knickpoints in valley networks; amphitheater-headed valleys, e.g. Nirgal Vallis area, Echus/Valles plateau margins), Earth (Niagara-type retreating knickpoints; Hawai'i amphitheater valleys; Box Canyon, Idaho) · engine→ _Drainage / stream-power incision engine — knickpoints are the transient/celerity term of the SAME incision law; the engine that builds F11's channels should emit knickpoint convexities + headward-retreat alcoves as a byproduct, gated on a base-level-fall event._ · src: 'Amphitheater-Headed Valley (Mars, Earth),' Encyclopedia of Planetary Landforms (Springer); Lamb et al. 2007, 'Formation of amphitheater-headed valleys by waterfall erosion after large-scale slumping on Hawai'i' (GSA Bulletin); Mars valley-network sapping vs runoff literature (Nirgal Vallis junction-angle studies)
- **Drainage network PATTERN types (dendritic / rectangular-structural / trellis / parallel / sapping-amphitheater)** · `OBS` — The PLAN-FORM topology of the whole valley network, which is diagnostic of the substrate and erosion mechanism, not just decoration. Dendritic = homogeneous substrate + surface runoff (branching tree). Rectangular & trellis = fracture/joint or fault structural control (right-angle junctions, e.g. Titan's structurally-controlled networks). Parallel = steep uniform regional slope. Sapping/amphitheater networks = stubby, blunt-headed, low junction-angle tributaries from groundwater seepage rather than rain. Baseline F11 lists only 'dendritic network' as one variant — it has no pattern-typology axis, yet the pattern is the single biggest read on the body's substrate + water source.
  - bodies: Titan (rectangular AND dendritic networks; structural control documented), Mars (dendritic valley networks; sapping/amphitheater networks like Nirgal Vallis), Earth (trellis Appalachians; rectangular jointed terrain; dendritic shales) · engine→ _Drainage / stream-power network engine, with the network TOPOLOGY selected by a substrate-anisotropy / structural-control driver (fault-fabric field) and an erosion-mechanism flag (runoff vs sapping) — one engine, several pattern presets._ · src: Burr et al. 2013 and follow-ups on Titan fluvial networks; 'Morphology of fluvial networks on Titan: Evidence for structural control' (ResearchGate / Icarus); Black et al. / Pieri groundwater-sapping junction-angle studies on Mars valley networks; Titan 'Fluvial and Lacustrine Landscapes' review, arXiv:2502.02556 (2025)
- **Meander-belt micro-morphology (oxbow lakes, scroll bars, point bars, neck/chute cutoffs, floodplain & interfluves)** · `OBS` — The fine-scale within-channel and floodplain forms of a MEANDERING river: actively migrating bends leave curved scroll-bar ridges (accreted inner-bank point bars), abandoned loops become crescent oxbow lakes, neck/chute cutoffs sever bends, and the surrounding flat floodplain + interfluves (the un-incised land between valleys) record overbank deposition. Baseline F11 lists 'meanders' as a single appearance variant with no sub-structure. On Mars (Aeolis Dorsa) these are spectacularly preserved (often as inverted scroll bars). They are the close-LOD2 texture that makes a meandering river read as a real river.
  - bodies: Mars (Aeolis Dorsa: oxbow lakes, neck and chute cutoffs, point-bar meander scrolls, scroll bars), Earth (any lowland meander belt), Titan (meandering/deposition channels reported in SAR) · engine→ _Drainage / channel-planform engine with a meander-migration sub-model (lateral accretion + cutoff dynamics) — the same trunk-channel that the network engine routes, given a migration/sinuosity stage that emits scrolls + oxbows on its floodplain._ · src: Matsubara et al. / Goudge work on Aeolis Dorsa; 'River meandering on Earth and Mars: A comparative study of Aeolis Dorsa meanders' (Geomorphology); NASA/HiRISE 'Martian Meanders and Scroll-Bars' (ESP_020673_1750, uahirise.org); Stratigraphy of Aeolis Dorsa, Mars, arXiv:1712.03951
- **Open- vs closed-basin lakes & breached-rim outlet spillways (lake hydrological topology)** · `OBS` — The connectivity STATE of a standing-liquid basin, which controls its whole appearance. Closed-basin lake = inlet valley breaches the rim, no outlet (water level set by evaporation; concentric strandlines, central playa). Open-basin lake = both inlet AND outlet valleys; the outlet is a deeply incised spillway/breach canyon cut by overflow flooding, and a closed topographic contour below the outlet shows incomplete drainage. Chains of such basins overflow downstream into each other (cascade lakes). Baseline F14 ('lakes & seas') and F20 ('coastlines') treat lakes as a fill level with islands — they have no inlet/outlet topology, no breach spillway, no overflow-cascade, which is the dominant Mars paleolake signal (250+ open basins catalogued).
  - bodies: Mars (Jezero open basin with Pliva Vallis outlet; Ma'adim Vallis source basins; 200+ closed-basin & 250+ open-basin paleolakes catalogued), Earth (Great Basin pluvial lake chains; Bonneville flood breach) · engine→ _Coupled drainage + hydrology / lake-routing engine (the lake-filling + outlet-routing step of the Braun-Willett-style drainage solver) — basin connectivity is an emergent property of routing flow over the relief, not a paint-on._ · src: Fassett & Head 2008, 'Valley network-fed, open-basin lakes on Mars,' Icarus; Goudge et al. 2012, 'Constraints on the history of open-basin lakes on Mars,' JGR Planets; Goudge et al. 2018/2019, 'Incision of paleolake outlet canyons on Mars from overflow flooding' (Geology); Candidate closed-basin lakes classification, Icarus 2015 (S0019103515003310)
- **Wave-cut coastal smoothing — beaches, barrier islands, wave-modified shorelines, sea cliffs** · `OBS` — The ACTIVE coastal-process suite where wave (or, on Titan, gentle wind-wave) energy reworks a shoreline: smooths it, builds sandy/sediment beaches and barrier-island bars, cuts wave-notch sea cliffs, and grades a wave-cut platform. Distinct from baseline F20's 'strandline / paleo-shoreline / sea cliff / beach' in that those are STATIC abandoned high-water marks, whereas this is the wave-energy ENGINE producing a characteristically smoothed, sorted coast in the present. Titan's Ontario Lacus shows a smooth wave-modified beach + putative barrier islands, and a 2024 study reports wave-erosion signatures in Titan's sea coasts. - **Ice-giant Great Dark Spot + companion (orographic) clouds** · `OBS` — On the ice giants, the giant vortex appears as a DARK ellipse (a clearing/downwelling that reveals deeper dark layers) rather than a bright/red oval, and it is capped by bright white 'companion clouds' of methane-ice that hover above and around it like lenticular caps over a mountain. These spots are short-lived (years), drift in latitude, and form/dissipate — a fundamentally different lifecycle and appearance from the centuries-stable Jovian Great Red Spot in F27. Our F27 is explicitly 'single GRS / dark spot' but does not capture the dark-spot-PLUS-bright-methane-companion morphology or the ice-giant lifecycle.

- **Convective-outbreak storm lifecycle (Great White Spot / Dragon Storm)** · `OBS` — A planetary-scale moist-convective eruption that begins as a compact bright 'head', then a turbulent tail unspools eastward/westward until it wraps the entire planet in a band of churned bright cloud, and finally decays — a time-evolving event, not a static feature. - **Layered / detached photochemical haze shells by chemistry (Titan-Pluto class)** · `OBS` — Cold N2/CH4 worlds build optically thick organic (tholin) haze from UV photochemistry that organizes into MULTIPLE concentric, sharply separated shells visible on the limb — notably a high 'detached haze layer' floating hundreds of km above the main deck, plus tens of fine sub-layers in Pluto's case. - **Cloud-species color/level differentiation by condensation chemistry** · `OBS` — Which molecule condenses sets BOTH the altitude of the visible deck AND its intrinsic color: ammonia ice (white), ammonium hydrosulfide (brown/orange), water ice (deep white), all stacked at different pressures; on hotter worlds silicate/iron/corundum/MnS/Na2S/Cr/KCl/ZnS clouds each condense at their own temperature, producing reflective grey-metallic to dusky decks. - **Orographic / topography-coupled clouds (lee waves, volcano caps, elongated leeward streamers)** · `OBS` — Where wind meets relief, clouds organize around topography: bright orographic caps over big volcanoes, lee-wave (lenticular) trains downwind of mountains, and dramatic single elongated leeward cloud streamers (the Arsia Mons cloud reaches ~1800 km westward). - **Mesospheric / noctilucent (high-altitude tenuous) clouds** · `OBS` — Faint, high, tenuous cloud sheets near the top of an atmosphere that catch light when the lower atmosphere is in shadow — Earth's noctilucent (polar mesospheric) clouds glow electric-blue at twilight near the terminator; Mars has high-altitude CO2-ice mesospheric clouds. - **Composition-dependent sky / limb color and twilight inversion** · `OBS` — The intrinsic COLOR of a planet's sky and limb is set by what the atmosphere is made of and what aerosols it carries, not just by scattering geometry: Uranus/Neptune skies are deep blue-green because methane absorbs red; a dusty Mars sky is butterscotch by day with an INVERTED blue glow around the sun at sunset (dust forward-scatters blue); Titan's sky is orange.   - bodies: Uranus, Neptune (methane blue-green sky), Mars (butterscotch day sky, blue sunset halo), Titan (orange sky), Earth (blue) · engine→ _Atmospheric-optics engine parameterized by gas composition + aerosol type: a Rayleigh/Mie scattering engine whose color output is keyed to the atmosphere's chemistry — the same optics engine behind F34/F35, extended to set base hue._ · src: Atmosphere of Venus / planetary atmospheres reviews (Wikipedia); Mars blue-sunset dust-scattering studies (MSL/Pathfinder imaging literature)
- **UV-absorber dark cloud markings (Venus-class 'unknown absorber' streaks)** · `OBS` — Venus's bland visible disk hides a vivid dark-and-light cloud pattern in the ultraviolet caused by a patchy, still-not-fully-identified UV absorber (candidates: iron chloride minerals, sulfur allotropes) concentrated in equatorial dark streaks and the planet-scale 'Y'. - **Stationary atmospheric gravity-wave bow features** · `OBS` — A surface feature (mountain/highland) can imprint a giant standing wave in the cloud deck far above it: Akatsuki saw a ~10,000 km bright bow-shaped feature on Venus that stayed FIXED over Aphrodite Terra highland while ~100 m/s winds streamed past it — the largest known stationary gravity wave. - **Polar hood / seasonal polar cloud cap (atmospheric, not surface frost)** · `OBS` — A persistent cloud/haze CAP over a winter pole — distinct from a surface frost cap (F22) and from a polar VORTEX storm (F29). - **Glory / opposition cloud-optic rings on a cloud deck** · `OBS` — A glory is a set of concentric colored rings centered on the anti-solar point, seen looking down onto a deck of uniform spherical droplets — Venus shows a glory at its sub-spacecraft point (Venus Express), confirming uniform sulfuric-acid droplet sizes. - **Patchy / inhomogeneous cloud cover with terminator-asymmetric clouds** · `SIM` — On hot Jupiters (and brown dwarfs) clouds are NOT uniform: reflective condensate clouds form preferentially over the cooler WESTERN limb / high latitudes and get advected by the super-rotating jet onto the dayside before evaporating, producing a measurable bright/dark hemispheric asymmetry and a phase-curve offset; brown dwarfs show patchy clearing/cloudy cells driving rotational brightness variability. - **Eyeball cloud-advection banding (rapid-rotator eyeball variant)** · `SIM` — On a tidally locked ocean world the substellar deep-convective cloud cap (captured in F31f) only stays a centered 'pupil' for SLOW rotators; for faster rotators the substellar cloud deck shifts eastward and stretches into banded cloud structures wrapping the globe — so the same eyeball climate produces either a centered cloud spot OR an off-center swept band depending on rotation rate. ### Impact / regolith (16)

- **Impact melt sheets, ponds, veneers & melt flows** · `OBS` — The shock-melted target rock that lines and surrounds a fresh complex crater: a coherent melt SHEET draping the floor, discrete melt PONDS perched on terraces and (preferentially) on the down-/cross-range exterior rim, thin melt VENEERS coating topography, and leveed, lobate melt FLOWS that drained downslope while molten and froze in place. Visually distinct from ejecta: smoother, darker/glassier, fills lows, has flow fronts and drainage channels. Largest ponds reach >7 km; the floor sheet at large craters (Copernicus, Tycho, King) is the dominant interior unit. Baseline F2 captures only basin HEIGHT and F3 captures ballistic debris — neither captures this molten-then-frozen unit, which is a different physical product (in-place quench melt vs. ballistic regolith). observed.
  - bodies: Moon (Copernicus, Tycho, King, Kepler, Glushko), Mercury, Mars (Hale) · engine→ _Impact-cratering engine (shock-melt sub-product): melt volume scales with impactor energy/target volatiles; the same single impact event that excavates the bowl also produces a melt budget that is partitioned into floor-sheet, rim-pond, veneer and flow by post-impact gravity drainage. - **Central pit / central dome / anomalous dome (icy-target interior morphologies)** · `OBS` — On icy satellites (Ganymede, Callisto) and Mars, mid-to-large complex craters do NOT form a rocky central PEAK; instead they show a central PIT (a deep summit pit, often floored by a smaller bowl), a central DOME (a raised hummock filling the pit), or an ANOMALOUS DOME (subdued, broad). These form a size-ordered sequence (pit -> dome -> anomalous dome up to ~170 km) driven by impact melt draining into sub-crater fractures and by topographic relaxation of a warm, weak ice layer aided by remnant impact heat. Baseline F2's central-peak/peak-ring sequence is the ROCKY-target branch only; the icy-target interior branch (pit/dome) is a parallel, physically distinct outcome of the same cratering engine on a volatile-rich, low-strength target.
  - bodies: Ganymede, Callisto, Mars (some large craters) · engine→ _Impact-cratering engine on a warm/weak (icy or ice-rich) target: target rheology + remnant impact heat + melt drainage replace gravity-driven peak rebound. - **Terraced inner walls & slump terraces (complex-crater wall collapse)** · `OBS` — The scalloped, stair-stepped inner walls of complex craters, formed when the over-steepened transient cavity wall collapses inward along listric normal faults during the modification stage, leaving concentric rotated wall blocks (terraces). ### Magnetospheric / auroral / radiation (11)

- **Lunar swirls (magnetic-anomaly albedo markings)** · `OBS` — Sinuous, looping bright-and-dark high-albedo patterns on airless bodies, co-located with crustal magnetic anomalies (the canonical case is Reiner Gamma on the Moon, plus Mare Ingenii and Gerasimovich swirls). ### Aeolian (wind) (10)

- **Megaripples / Transverse Aeolian Ridges (TARs)** · `OBS` — An intermediate-scale bedform between centimetre impact ripples and slip-faced dunes: symmetric in profile, no slip face, decametre wavelength (typ. ~25 m wavelength, ~90 m long, ~1 m tall on Mars). - **Wind-erosion sculpture field (yardangs + deflation: ridge-and-groove, scour pits, inverted relief)** · `OBS` — An EROSIONAL wind engine, opposite in sign to dune deposition: wind abrasion + deflation carves streamlined, keel-shaped ridges (yardangs) aligned to the prevailing wind out of weakly-cemented fine-grained rock. ### Tectonic / orogenic / relief (13)

- **Global-contraction shortening fabric (thrust/lobate-scarp belts + wrinkle-ridge province as ONE engine)** · `OBS` — A planet-wide, quasi-uniform population of contractional landforms produced by whole-body radius decrease (interior cooling) and/or tidal despinning, NOT plate collision. The lithosphere fails in shortening everywhere at once: lobate scarps (curved thrust-fault steps, e.g. Mercury's Discovery/Beagle Rupes, ~3 km relief, hundreds of km long), interconnected with broad low wrinkle ridges over volcanic plains, and small grabens where the same global stress flips locally extensional. The signature outcome is a GLOBAL fabric with a preferred orientation (N-S-favored near equator from despinning, random near poles) rather than a localized belt — this is the dominant relief style of Mercury and a major one on the Moon and Mars. The catalog's F5 lists 'lobate contraction scarp' and 'wrinkle ridge' as menu items inside a generic fault grab-bag, but it has NO concept of the global-contraction ENGINE that co-produces them planet-wide and orients them — they read as scattered local cliffs rather than one shrinking-planet signature. This is the single biggest missing tectonic engine: it should drive scarp DISTRIBUTION and ORIENTATION across the whole sphere, gated on a cooling/airless body.

- **Rift system with flexural shoulder uplift (the rift-flank highlands are PART of the rift, not a separate plateau)** · `OBS` — A continental-style rift is not just a graben low — mechanical unloading of the footwall plus thermal buoyancy lifts the rift FLANKS 3-5 km above the rift floor, so the rift simultaneously generates a linear low AND the bordering highlands/escarpments. The East African Rift is the type example (aligned tectonic basins separated by shoals, bordered by uplifted shoulders); Valles Marineris is the Mars analog as the surface expression of Tharsis-radial extension, with bounding scarps and uplifted plateau margins. - **Tessera structural sub-types (ribbon, basin-and-dome/honeycomb, S-/SR-ridged) as distinct deformation fabrics** · `OBS` — The catalog's F6 has 'crustal-plateau tessera' as a single line item, but tessera is not one texture — it is a family of co-existing fabrics recording superposed deformation phases, and the SUB-TYPE is the visually distinguishing thing. - **Strike-slip / transcurrent fault systems as a distinct structural class (horizontal offset, en-echelon, tailcracks, pull-aparts)** · `OBS` — The catalog's F5 covers normal faults (cliffs), contraction (lobate scarps, wrinkle ridges), and horst-graben — i.e. the dip-slip families — but has NO strike-slip class. Strike-slip faults move horizontally and produce a diagnostic visual vocabulary: lateral offset of pre-existing features, en-echelon (stepped) crack arrays, tailcracks at fault tips, pull-apart basins at releasing bends, and pressure ridges at restraining bends. - **Tectonic giant polygons (kilometer-scale, fault-bounded — distinct from thermal-contraction patterned ground)** · `OBS` — Two utterly different engines make polygons and the catalog conflates them. - **Global expansion / freeze-extension fracture belt (interior-freezing crack network)** · `OBS` — When a body's interior (subsurface ocean) freezes, the resulting volume increase forces the rigid crust to crack to accommodate ~1% areal extension — producing a globe-girdling system of grabens, scarps and chasmata, typically an equatorial belt, with no compressional counterpart. Astron. (2020).*
- **Seasonal/transient surface darkening-brightening cycles (RSL, frost-cycle albedo)** — `observed` — *time-varying* markings: RSL streaks darkening/fading seasonally, frost flush, dust-clearing albedo swings; F18/F22/F40 capture the static landforms, not the transient temporal modality itself. — Mars — engine→ seasonal albedo cycle driven by D3+D1 — *McEwen et al., Science (2011).*

### Uncovered classes

1. **Surface chemistry / coloration as a feature class** — landform-rich but color-poor: names *where* material is, rarely *what color and why* (Io sulfur allotropes, Europa color-center salts, Mars evaporite brightness, pigment biosignatures).
2. **Ring–planet *interaction* effects** — rings are static bands; the ring's shadow on the disk, spokes, ring-rain inflow band are missing.
3. **Biosignature surface expressions** — biotic track is only bioluminescent/fungal; no red-edge land tint, ocean-color blooms, seasonal greening.
4. **Technosignature expressions beyond visible light** — tech track is structures + nightside lights; no waste-heat IR, in-orbit artificial shadowing, pollution haze.
5. **Exospheric / escape *visuals*** — escape is a gate (P25) + disintegrating-rock tails (#30), not the comet-like gas-escape tail of an intact evaporating world.
6. **Compact-object / exotic-host illumination class** — pulsar/neutron-star planets, and the lighting regime of non-stellar/pulsed sources, absent.
7. **Signature single-body terrains that resist generic-process framing** — Mercury hollows, Venus arachnoids/novae, Miranda disrupt-and-reassemble + mega-scarp, Umbriel bright cold-trap ring; the process-first model dissolves these into broad processes, but they deserve named hooks.
8. **The *temporal/transient* modality itself** — seasonal RSL, recurring frost, dust-clearing, spoke cycles — features defined by *change over time*, a cross-cutting class the snapshot-oriented sweep barely registers.
9. **Whole-disk compositional provinces** — latitudinal/hemispheric albedo-and-composition patterning (Titan Xanadu/maria, Io equatorial-vs-polar sulfur) as a *global organizing layer* above individual landforms.

### Coverage verdict

With F1–F53 plus the 129 additions, the catalog is **near-complete on solid-surface geomorphology** — impact, all volcanism modes, tectonics, fluvial, aeolian, glacial/sublimation, cryo, and the gas-giant band/storm/cloud stack are thoroughly covered, including most named signature terrains (Triton cantaloupe, Enceladus tiger stripes, Venus tessera, Mercury lobate scarps, Mars spiders/RSL/swiss-cheese, Titan dunes/labyrinth/lakes). The remaining gaps are **not in morphology but in *modality***: surface coloration/chemistry, ring-interaction effects, biosignature and beyond-visible technosignature expressions, exospheric/escape visuals, compact-object lighting, and the time-varying/seasonal and whole-disk-province layers. Adding the ~21 residuals above closes those modality gaps. **Estimated completeness: ~90% before these residuals, ~97–98% after** — the largest *remaining* uncertainty being the sf-speculative technosignature space, which is open-ended rather than physically bounded.
