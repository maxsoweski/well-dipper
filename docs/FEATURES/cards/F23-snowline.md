# Feature Card — F23 Snowline / frost-coverage boundary
Domain: Cryo · Lab status: ✅ · Build-seq phase: 3

## 1. Description (WHAT)

F23 "Snowline / frost-coverage boundary" (family F-volatile-surface — "the bridge family between climate and terrain"). Derives from P22 Seasonal volatile cycling: volatiles condense onto the cold pole as frost and sublimate in spring; F23 is the surface expression of the snowline itself — the boundary where local temperature crosses the volatile's condensation point. Physical chain: D1 equilibriumTemperature (T_eq, the master gate for volatile state and snowlines) sets the baseline; D2 volatileFraction sets the frost budget; D3 axialTilt sets the latitude of frost lines (high obliquity spreads frost to low latitudes, Mars-like); D5 atmosphere density modulates transport. Variants per the inventory: sharp coverage line · diffuse tint · latitude band vs. altitude band (frost climbs mountains). Real-body examples: Earth's altitude snowline on mountain ranges, Mars' retreating seasonal CO2 frost edge (~67°N in northern spring), with Triton's eroded N2 cap fringe as the sibling-F22 extreme. WD types: rocky, ice, terrestrial, eyeball. Intensity axis (P22): thin frost rim … cap reaching mid-latitudes. Note: the inventory table still tags F23 `[aspirational]`, but the lab code is ahead of the doc — it is fully built (see §2).

## 4. Math / modeling notes (HOW, from the field)

Academia models the snowline as an energy-balance isotherm: the elevation/latitude where annual ablation = accumulation, first-order approximated by the locus where local temperature crosses the volatile's frost point. Latitudinal insolation gives T(lat) ≈ T_eq scaled by a cos-latitude factor; altitude subtracts an adiabatic lapse (~6.5 K/km for Earth air), so the snowline is a tilted isotherm sheet intersecting topography — equatorial mountains carry snowcaps above the same isotherm that sits at sea level near the poles. For Mars-type CO2 frost the canonical model is the Leighton–Murray CO2 energy-balance cycle (frost stable where surface T drops below the ~150 K frost point at ~6 mbar); Triton/Pluto N2 use volatile-transport models (e.g. the arXiv 2110.11992 lineage) where the frost line migrates with the subsolar point. Obliquity sets how far equatorward the seasonal cap reaches. Games/procedural systems collapse all of this into a scalar 'temperature field' — temperature = base − latitudeFactor − height·lapse — thresholded for snow (Minecraft-style biome temp, Sebastian Lague's biome shading, terrain shaders that add slope-dependent snow retention via dot(N,up)). In the research doc's vocabulary this is a pure coverage/albedo feature: it lives in LUMINANCE, the one channel the 6-level Bayer posterize rewards ("route detail through normals/specular... detail that moves the lit value survives"), with the edge as a smoothstep band whose width maps to a dither-gradient zone, and analytic `noised()` supplying domain-offset fractal breakup of the iso-line. Most promising shader-side approach (and what is built): evaluate localT = T_eq·(1 − chill·coldFactor) − h·lapse·T_eq per fragment, smoothstep a Kelvin-width band around the species condensation point with FBM breakup added in Kelvin units, and mix the base albedo toward a bright species-tinted frostAlbedo BEFORE posterize so the cap quantizes to a clean bright step. The natural next refinements are seasonal animation (drive condensationT or the cold-point axis with orbital phase for advance/retreat) and slope-dependent retention (damp coverage by the analytic gradient so steep faces shed frost), both cheap given the gradient is already available from noised().

## 6. What to judge (UAT checklist)

- [ ] Does the cap read as a coherent bright polar (or antistellar) region — a clean one-to-two-step luminance lift in the 6-level posterized envelope — rather than a mere hue shift that quantization crushes?
- [ ] Does the snowline edge read as a natural ragged margin in the dither, not a drawn-on latitude circle — does the fractal breakup show at mid distance without dissolving the boundary into noise?
- [ ] Does frost climb mountains — bright summits appearing equatorward of the main cap where relief is high (altitude-lapse behavior), so the boundary visibly tracks terrain instead of floating over it?
- [ ] Does the softness knob span both inventory variants — a sharp coverage line at low softness vs. a diffuse dither-gradient tint band at high softness — without posterize banding artifacts in between?
- [ ] On a tidally-locked preset, does the cap re-anchor to the antistellar cold point as an eyeball nightside cap (terminator-aligned edge) instead of a geographic polar cap?
- [ ] Does high axial tilt spread frost convincingly toward low latitudes while zero tilt holds a tight, pole-symmetric cap — i.e., does the D3 driver read as cap geometry, not just cap size?
- [ ] Do the species tints behave — water white, CO2 grey-white, CH4 tholin-pink, N2 blue-white — with the luminance lift load-bearing and the tint reading as flavor through the posterize?
- [ ] Regression behavior: with frost budget 0 or a hot world (Lava preset), is the base surface pixel-identical — no residual rim or tint at the would-be snowline?
