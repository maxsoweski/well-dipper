# Well Dipper — Music Inspiration Examples

> Reference listening for the five tracks that still need composing
> (per `Well-Dipper-Music-Guide.docx` and
> `docs/ARCHIVE/COMPOSER_REFERENCE_LEGACY.md`). Each example is a real,
> findable piece — search the quoted string on YouTube or Spotify.
> None of these are "copy this"; they're vibe anchors to browse, keep,
> or veto.

Game-wide register, for orientation: meditative late-90s retro synth —
sparse, warm, vast rather than frantic. Touchstones already named in the
Bible: Outer Wilds, FTL, Katamari Damacy, No Man's Sky, Stellaris. The
autopilot register is "Blue Danube over 2001's docking sequence."

---

## 1. `hyperspace` — warp tunnel loop

**Descriptor:** Fast, intense, rhythmic — retro sci-fi energy, 120–140
BPM. Being *pulled* by the tunnel, not thrusting. Propulsive but not
combat-tense — wonder, not threat. Relativistic blue-shift register.

| Example | Why it fits | Search |
|---|---|---|
| CoLD SToRAGE — "Cairodrome" (Wipeout 2097, PS1) | The exact era and the exact feel: propulsive PS1 electronica, speed as joy not danger | `cold storage cairodrome wipeout` |
| CoLD SToRAGE — "Canada" (Wipeout, PS1) | Same composer, slightly dreamier — speed with a floaty top layer | `cold storage canada wipeout` |
| Keiichi Sugiyama — "Buggie Running Beeps 01" (Rez, Dreamcast) | Trance that *builds wonder* as you fly down a tunnel — Rez is literally a rail-tunnel game from this hardware era | `rez buggie running beeps` |
| Hiroshi Kawaguchi — "Magical Sound Shower" (OutRun, Sega arcade) | The cheerful end of the spectrum: pure speed-as-delight, zero threat | `outrun magical sound shower` |
| M.O.O.N. — "Hydrogen" (Hotline Miami) | Modern synthwave propulsion — darker, heavier; good for calibrating how far toward "intense" we want to go | `moon hydrogen hotline miami` |
| Daft Punk — "End of Line" (TRON: Legacy) | Mid-tempo electronic pull — the "carried by the machine" feeling rather than driving it | `daft punk end of line` |
| John Williams — "Hyperspace" (The Empire Strikes Back OST) | The orchestral archetype of the jump itself — racing strings, momentum without combat | `empire strikes back hyperspace track` |

## 2. `warp-charge` — 6–7s sting under the portal opening

**Descriptor:** Building tension, escalating energy. Crescendo into the
tunnel. Dangerous/exciting — "tearing a hole in space." Layers over the
existing sub-bass/sawtooth SFX, so the music sting rides on top.

| Example | Why it fits | Search |
|---|---|---|
| Hans Zimmer — "No Time for Caution" (Interstellar, docking scene) | The gold standard of an escalating organ/ostinato build that resolves into action | `interstellar no time for caution` |
| Hans Zimmer — "Supermarine" (Dunkirk) — first 60s | Built on a **Shepard tone**: a riser that seems to climb forever. The technique itself is worth stealing for a 6s sting | `dunkirk supermarine shepard tone` |
| Richard Strauss — "Also sprach Zarathustra" opening (2001) | Three-note crescendo into a massive hit — the original "something enormous is about to happen" | `also sprach zarathustra 2001` |
| Star Trek (2009) — warp jump moments | Sound-design reference more than music: the held breath, then the *punch*. Our hard-cut into hyperspace works the same way | `star trek 2009 warp jump scene` |
| Geinoh Yamashirogumi — "Kaneda" (Akira) — opening build | Escalation via percussion and voices instead of synths — a weirder, more organic option for "reality tearing" | `akira kaneda theme` |

## 3. `arrival` — 3–5s "we're here" sting

**Descriptor:** Brief wonder, relief after hyperspace intensity. The
musical comma between the tunnel and the system's explore bed.

| Example | Why it fits | Search |
|---|---|---|
| Zelda: Ocarina of Time — "Secret discovered" chime | The canonical 2-second "the world just opened" jingle — bright, resolved, gone before it overstays | `zelda ocarina secret discovered sound` |
| Metroid — item acquisition jingle | Slightly more mysterious flavor of the same move — wonder with an alien edge | `metroid item get jingle` |
| Super Mario 64 — Power Star fanfare | The triumphant end of the spectrum — probably *too* celebratory for us, but useful as a boundary marker | `mario 64 star get fanfare` |
| John Williams — "Journey to the Island" reveal swell (Jurassic Park) | The film-scoring archetype of an arrival reveal: one warm brass swell as the new world appears | `jurassic park journey to the island` |
| No Man's Sky — discovery stings | Same genre, same moment: short synth shimmer when something new is catalogued | `no man's sky discovery sound` |

## 4. `deepsky` — galaxies, nebulae, clusters loop

**Descriptor:** Grander, more awe-inspiring — bigger sense of scale than
explore. Not scarier, just *larger*. Camera parked outside an entire
galaxy or a 12-billion-year-old cluster.

| Example | Why it fits | Search |
|---|---|---|
| Vangelis — Cosmos theme (from "Heaven and Hell," used in Carl Sagan's Cosmos) | The definitive "beholding the galaxy" synth piece — vast, warm, reverent | `vangelis cosmos carl sagan theme` |
| Sam Hulick / Jack Wall — "Uncharted Worlds" (Mass Effect galaxy map) | Literally the music for browsing a galaxy — synth pads that signal scale without urgency | `mass effect uncharted worlds` |
| Andreas Waldetoft — "In Search of Life" (Stellaris) | Already a named touchstone for the game — grand sweeping space synths | `stellaris in search of life` |
| Hans Zimmer — "Stay" or "Cornfield Chase" (Interstellar) | Organ as scale: huge but emotionally warm rather than threatening | `interstellar stay hans zimmer` |
| Gustav Holst — "Neptune, the Mystic" (The Planets) | The orchestral root of all "deep space awe" — distant, wordless choir fading into the void | `holst neptune the mystic` |
| Samuel Barber — Adagio for Strings (Homeworld arrangement) | Grief-adjacent vastness — how Homeworld made "ancient and enormous" feel sacred | `homeworld adagio for strings` |
| Jón Hallur — "Below the Asteroids" (EVE Online) | Ambient grandeur designed to be lived in for hours — closest functional match to a deepsky loop | `eve online below the asteroids` |

## 5. `explore-alt` — variation on the explore bed (LOW priority)

**Descriptor:** Same key/mood as `explore` — open, unhurried, vast,
meditative wonder — but different melody or instruments. These are
anchors for the *family*, useful for explore-flavored variations
generally (including the future per-system variants).

| Example | Why it fits | Search |
|---|---|---|
| Brian Eno — "An Ending (Ascent)" (Apollo: Atmospheres and Soundtracks) | The archetype of meditative space drift — the whole Apollo album is required listening for this slot | `brian eno an ending ascent` |
| Brian Eno — "Deep Blue Day" (same album) | Same drift but with gentle *movement* — pedal steel over pads, warmth in zero-g | `brian eno deep blue day` |
| C418 — "Subwoofer Lullaby" / "Wet Hands" (Minecraft) | Sparse piano over soft pads — exactly the "Roland piano + GarageBand synths" combo the Music Guide suggests | `c418 subwoofer lullaby` |
| Ben Prunty — "MilkyWay (Explore)" / "Civil (Explore)" (FTL) | Named touchstone — calm synth exploration that survives hundreds of repeats | `ftl milkyway explore` |
| 65daysofstatic — "Hypersleep" (No Man's Sky) | Named touchstone, ambient side — procedural-feeling texture that never demands attention | `65daysofstatic hypersleep` |
| Boards of Canada — "Olson" or "Dayvan Cowboy" | Warm, slightly degraded analog synth — the *sound* of the late-90s aesthetic in music form | `boards of canada dayvan cowboy` |
| Yuu Miyake et al. — "Lonely Rolling Star" (Katamari Damacy) | The game's visual-style touchstone — if explore-alt wants a playful tilt, this is what that sounds like | `katamari lonely rolling star` |

## Browsing notes

- Search strings are plain YouTube/Spotify queries — top result is the
  right one for all of these.
- For loops meant to be heard for hours (`explore-alt`, `deepsky`,
  `hyperspace`), listen to candidates twice in a row and notice what
  gets annoying — that's the most useful signal to pass to Christian.
- The per-system mood families (red dwarf, halo, emission nebula, etc.,
  §3 of the composer reference) aren't covered here — that's a later,
  bigger curation pass once `explore` is locked.
